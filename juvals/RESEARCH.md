## 1. Google reviews, NFC cards and reputation


TOPIC BRIEF: Google Business Profile, Google reviews and reputation for Juval's Grill & Restaurant (Camalaniugan, Cagayan). Researched 4 October 2026.

Note on what this builds on: Juval's Google figures (4.1 stars, 27 reviews) and the client facts come from the task brief. I could not open Juval's live Google or Facebook listing (a search turned up nothing), so the current numbers should be re-read from the profile on the day of the pitch.

=====================================================
1) KEY FACTS AND NUMBERS (with sources)
=====================================================

How shoppers use reviews (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026: 1,002 US adults, published 11 Feb 2026, https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)
- 97% read reviews for local businesses. 41% "always" read them, up from 29% in 2025.
- Rating floors: 68% need at least 4 stars (55% in 2025). 31% use only businesses rated 4.5 or higher (17% in 2025). 10% demand 5.0.
- Volume: 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Juval's, at 27, is only just over that line.
- Recency: 74% look for reviews from the last 3 months. 32% look for reviews from the last 2 weeks (20% in 2025).
- Replies: 80% are more likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews. 50% are put off by generic or templated replies. 81% expect a reply within a week, and 19% expect one the same day (6% in 2025).
- Sources: Google 71% (83% in 2025). ChatGPT and other AI tools 45% (6% in 2025). 82% read AI-generated review summaries, and 23% decide on the summary alone.
- Caveat: this is a US sample. No equivalent Philippine survey was found (UNVERIFIED for PH).

Local ranking (Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, published 6 Nov 2025, https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Review factors in the Local Pack/Maps ranking: #6 High numerical Google rating (4 to 5), #9 Quantity of native Google reviews with text, #11 Recency of reviews, #14 Sustained influx of reviews over time rather than bursts, #36 Keywords in reviews, #43 Reviews with photos. Owner responses to most reviews rank much lower, at #122.
- Whitespark says "the biggest changes we're seeing are an increased importance of review signals and behavioural signals."
- Group weights for the Local Pack, as reported by secondary sites summarising the chart: GBP signals 32%, Review signals 20% (16% in 2023), On-page 15%, Behavioral 9%, Links 8%, Citations 6%, Personalization 6%, Social 4% (https://www.centricdxb.com/insights/local-seo-ranking-factors). The Whitespark page shows these only as a chart, so the exact percentages are UNVERIFIED against the primary source.
- Google's own wording: prominence is "based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." Distance is the other big factor, so Juval's mainly competes for searches made near Camalaniugan and along the Aparri to Lal-lo highway (https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

Rating and revenue
- Luca (Harvard Business School, "Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of Yelp.com", Seattle data 2003 to 2009): a one-star rise in Yelp rating led to a 5 to 9 percent rise in revenue. The effect held only for independent restaurants, not chains (https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2011/10/hbs-study-finds-positive-yelp-reviews-lead-to-increased-business). This is US data on Yelp, not Google, so treat it as directional.
- Anderson and Magruder (Economic Journal, 300 San Francisco restaurants): going from 3.5 to 4 displayed stars raised the chance of selling out at prime time from 34% to 54% (https://sfist.com/2012/09/04/_half_a_star_can_make_a_big_differe.php).
- Proserpio and Zervas (Marketing Science, TripAdvisor hotels): hotels that started replying got 12% more reviews, and their ratings rose an average of 0.12 stars (https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Want-to-improve-your-business-s-online-ratings-Make-sure-to-respond-to-reviews).

Enforcement (Google)
- 2025: Google blocked or removed 292 million policy-violating reviews and 13 million fake Business Profiles, and put posting restrictions on 783,000 accounts. It reported 21% more fake reviews removed than the year before (report dated 17 Apr 2026: https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-spam-fighting-2025-41176.html, https://searchengineland.com/google-credits-gemini-for-better-detection-of-fake-business-reviews-and-maps-spam-454019).
- July 2026: an aggressive spam sweep wiped reviews from thousands of profiles and paused new reviews on some. One business fell from about 4,651 reviews to 63 in 24 hours. Google confirmed an investigation on 3 Jul 2026 and promised to restore reviews removed in error (https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-business-profile-reviews-missing-investigation-2026). The lesson is that sudden spikes are risky even when honest.

Philippine channel context (DataReportal Digital 2026 Philippines, https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-philippines)
- 98.0M internet users (83.8% of the population).
- 95.8M social media user identities (Oct 2025).
- Messenger ad reach 65.8M.
- Median mobile download speed 59.64 Mbps (Aug 2025).
- NapoleonCat counts 109.3M Facebook users in Feb 2026 (https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-philippines/2026/02/).

Google tools for asking for reviews
- Google publishes an official review link and QR code. In the Business Profile go to Read reviews, then Get more reviews, then Share. The QR code can only be generated in a desktop browser. Google suggests putting the link on receipts, in chats and on printed QR signs (https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en).
- The feature launched in March 2025 and was documented on 31 Dec 2025 (https://ppc.land/google-formalizes-review-requests-nine-months-after-feature-launch/).

NFC technology
- NTAG213 holds 144 bytes (about 130 URL characters), enough for a Google review link or a short redirect. NTAG215 holds 504 bytes. Both are NFC Forum Type 2 (https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/NTAG213_215_216.pdf, https://www.wakdev.com/en/knowledge-base/nfc-chips/nxp-ntag213.html).
- iPhone XS and newer read URL tags in the background with no app. A banner appears and the guest must tap it. Only the first URL record is used (https://gototags.com/help/ios/nfc/reading/background).
- Android reads tags only while the screen is unlocked and NFC is switched on in Settings (https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/connectivity/nfc/nfc).
- Many budget phones popular in PH list NFC as "market/region dependent", for example the Redmi 15C (https://www.gsmarena.com/xiaomi_redmi_15c-14157.php) and the Infinix Smart 10 (https://m.gsmarena.com/infinix_smart_10-13933.php). So a QR code must sit next to every NFC tag. The share of Juval's guests whose phones have working NFC is UNVERIFIED.

2) WHAT BEST-IN-CLASS LOOKS LIKE (features worth copying)
=====================================================
- Toast Guest Feedback (https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Guest-Feedback?lang=en_US): stars plus tap-to-pick reasons, collected at checkout on handhelds and from digital receipts. Each submission is tied to the check, server and table. Managers get an SMS or email alert for negative feedback only, and there is a report filterable by server and table plus 30-day themes. Worth copying: the private feedback loop tied to the check and server, and the instant alert on a bad rating. Do not copy any flow that shows the Google link only to happy guests.
- Podium (https://fitsmallbusiness.com/podium-review): staff send a review invite by text after the visit, with one-tap links, reminders to people who have not responded, and replies from a single inbox. Worth copying: a one-tap link sent over the channel guests actually use. For Juval's that channel is Messenger or SMS to reservation, event and bilao customers.
- Birdeye (https://capterra.com/p/152997/BirdEye/): review requests triggered by the system after a transaction, plus AI-drafted replies. Worth copying: the POS triggering the request automatically. Skip: the subscription, which costs far more than a single provincial restaurant needs (Birdeye's own pricing UNVERIFIED).
- Popmenu, through its April 2026 Yext partnership (https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1614537/popmenu-partners-with-yext-to-help-restaurants-easily-manage-their-online-reputation-across-70-platforms): AI replies in the brand's voice for positive reviews, so staff time goes to the negative ones. Worth copying: owner-approved draft replies. Google itself is testing AI-suggested replies inside GBP for some accounts (https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-test-reply-to-reviews-with-ai-472167).
- Square and Owner.com: no specific review features were verified in this pass (UNVERIFIED). Their general model of putting the review link on every digital receipt is matched by Google's own guidance above.
- Google itself, which is free and the most important: menu editor, including an experimental AI conversion of a menu photo or PDF into a structured menu, live in 24 to 48 hours (https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455840?hl=en). Popular dish names and photos can be managed from the Maps app (https://support.google.com/business/answer/9322475?hl=en). Local business links take one menu link and up to 10 links each for reservations and food orders, and a preferred provider is shown first (https://support.google.com/reserve/answer/12174393?hl=en). Google reports that profiles with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks (quoted from Google's GBP help by https://www.dacgroup.com/insights/local-search-news/why-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-matter/; primary page not re-verified). Posts come as Update, Offer and Event types. The highlighted "What's happening" placement for restaurants is limited to the US, CA, AU and NZ, so PH availability is UNVERIFIED (https://www.dacgroup.com/en-ca/?p=134721).

3) PITFALLS, RULES AND COMPLIANCE
=====================================================
Google Maps user content policy (https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en). Verbatim points:
- Merchants may not "Offer incentives - such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review."
- Merchants may not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." This is review gating. A "Did you enjoy your meal? Yes goes to Google, No goes to the manager" flow is gating.
- "merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request that specific content be included."
- Explicitly banned: "Merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews" (staff quotas) and "Merchants requesting that staff solicit reviews that include specific content, including content that identifies a staff member." Asking guests to "mention Ana in your review" is therefore against policy.
- Conflict of interest: reviews from current or former employees, family or contractors are not allowed. Owners, staff and relatives must not review Juval's or Lucia's.
- "Unusual volumes or patterns of review contributions" are treated as manipulation. Whitespark separately scores a sustained influx above bursts.
- Allowed: soliciting genuine reviews with no incentive and no attempt to influence the rating or content.

Penalties (https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en)
- No new reviews for a set period.
- Existing reviews unpublished for a set period.
- A public warning that fake reviews were removed.
- Google emails the owner first, and the owner can appeal. Repeated or severe violations can lead to suspension (same policy page).

Profile structure rules (https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)
- "There should only be one profile per business." Separate profiles are for distinct co-located businesses (Google's example is a restaurant inside a hotel) or distinct departments.
- Implication for Juval's: Juval's Grill, Don Jose Pizza & Pasta and Rosalina Cafe share a venue, owners and probably staff. Keep one profile and list the three kitchens in the menu, the description and the photos. A separate Rosalina Cafe profile is defensible only if it has its own signage, counter, hours and operations; otherwise it risks a suspension (judgement call).
- Lucia's is a different business or location, so it should have its own profile and its own review link.
- Keep the name exactly as on the signage. Adding words like "Best Restaurant Aparri" violates the name guideline.

Other traps
- NFC alone fails on phones without NFC or with NFC switched off, so always print the QR code too.
- Cheap marketplace tags arrive blank or carry the seller's demo URL. Write and lock the tags after testing; an unlocked tag can be rewritten by anyone with a free app (standard NTAG behaviour; see the datasheet above).
- Do not run a "review night" at a 150-guest event. A burst from a single event can trip the spam filters (inference from the policy text and the July 2026 sweep).
- GBP chat and call history were shut down on 31 Jul 2024 (https://ppc.land/google-business-profile-chat-feature-to-sunset-on-july-31-2024). Messenger stays the conversation channel.
- Data Privacy Act (RA 10173): only send Messenger or SMS review requests to customers who gave their number for that order and were told it may be used for follow-up. Keep one message, no repeats (general DPA consent principle; the specific NPC guidance was not checked in this pass, UNVERIFIED).
- Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967, fully enforced from 20 Jun 2025) targets deceptive practices by online merchants. Secondary sources say it covers fake reviews (https://batasnatin.com/laws/commercial-ecom-internet-transactions-act-RA-11967, https://tribune.net.ph/2025/06/29/dti-starts-e-commerce-laws-full-enforcement). The exact clause wording is UNVERIFIED (the primary text was not readable).
- Facebook: Pages use Recommend / Don't recommend in place of stars (since Aug 2018), with a 25-character minimum (https://www.birdeye.com/blog/facebook-recommendations-update/). The Reviews tab can be switched off in Page settings; keep it on (https://success.hearsaysocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/216863127-Turning-Ratings-and-Reviews-on-off-for-Facebook-Business-Pages). Meta's own rule on incentivized recommendations was not checked (UNVERIFIED); apply the same no-incentive rule.
- TripAdvisor: a search for restaurants in Aparri and Cagayan returned only Trip.com listings, with little TripAdvisor presence for the area (https://uk.trip.com/travel-guide/destination/cagayan-1474348/). In the 2023 BrightLocal data, 29% of US consumers used TripAdvisor (as shown at https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/2026/). Low priority for a highway restaurant whose diners are mostly local: claim the listing if it exists and spend no effort there.

4) CONCRETE RECOMMENDATIONS FOR JUVAL'S
=====================================================
Growth model assumptions (all UNVERIFIED; owners to confirm):
- About 100 guests a day across the three kitchens, averaging 2.7 per party. That is about 37 parties a day, or about 1,100 parties a month.
- About 60 reservation, event or bilao customers a month leave a phone number or a Messenger thread.
- The current 4.1 rating sits on about 110.7 star-points (4.1 x 27).
- Conversion: vendor data says 15 to 28% of people who scan a review QR on a stand complete a review (RecensioAI user data, no stated methodology, https://recensio-ai.lovable.app/blog/google-review-link-or-qr-code-which-works-better-for-more-reviews). I apply a much lower share of parties who scan in the first place.

Scenarios (new reviews per month):
- Conservative, stands only and nobody asks: 0.5% of parties, about 6 a month.
- Base, stands plus a polite ask from staff at every table when the bill is delivered, plus one follow-up message to reservation, event and bilao customers: 1.2% of parties (about 13) plus about 2 to 6 from messages, so about 15 a month.
- Strong, base plus a review card in every bilao and catering delivery: about 25 a month.

Projected total review count:
| | Month 3 | Month 6 | Month 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 45 | 63 | about 99 |
| Base | 72 | 117 | about 207 |
| Strong | 102 | 177 | about 327 |

Rating path in the base case, if new reviews average 4.6:
- Month 4 (87 reviews): about 4.4.
- Month 7 (132 reviews): about 4.5, which clears the 4.5-star filter used by 31% of consumers.
- If new reviews average only 4.4, the displayed rating stalls at about 4.3. In that case fix the complaints the private feedback surfaces before pushing for more volume.

How many reviews to compete: there is no published PH benchmark.
- Practical targets: pass 50 within 3 months, 100 or more within 6 to 9 months, and never let the newest review be more than 2 weeks old (BrightLocal recency data).
- Before the pitch, record the review counts of the top 3 local-pack results for "restaurant Aparri", "restaurant Camalaniugan", "pancit batil patong", "pizza Aparri" and "milk tea Aparri". Competitor counts are UNVERIFIED.

What to build or set up:
1. GBP clean-up, one day, PHP 0 (our labour only). Expected impact: high, since GBP signals are the top ranking group at a reported 32%.
   - Verify ownership. Exact name from signage. Primary category restaurant (Filipino); secondary categories for pizza, cafe, caterer and event venue only as needed ("fewest categories").
   - Hours, plus special hours for Undas, Christmas and New Year.
   - Description naming all three kitchens.
   - Menu through the menu editor (AI photo or PDF import), with popular dishes named by the owner: pancit batil patong, sisig, crispy pata, bilao trays, pizza, milk tea.
   - Local business links: menu link to the website menu, reservation link to the website inquiry form, order link to the Messenger or website order.
   - Attributes: outdoor/garden seating, good for kids, groups, payment options.
   - 30 or more real photos: food from each kitchen, the garden, the rattan hall, the facade from the highway.
   - Weekly post, for example the bilao of the week or an event-hall promo.
   - A separate profile for Lucia's.
2. Review link and QR, half a day, PHP 0. Generate them in a desktop browser. Put a short redirect on the website we already built (for example /review) so printed materials survive any link change and taps can be counted without collecting personal data.
3. Tap-or-scan table tents and bill folders, half a day, about PHP 3,500 to 7,500. Assumes 20 tables at PHP 150 to 250 for a local acrylic print plus a PHP 10 sticker (UNVERIFIED), 2 counter stands (Grill cashier, Rosalina counter), and 6 server cards at about PHP 142 each. Expected impact: medium.
   - Wording example: "Kumain ka dito? Tell others on Google. Tap or scan." Never "5 stars please".
   - NTAG213 or NTAG215, written and locked, with a large QR beside every chip.
4. Per-server cards, kept within policy.
   - Each server carries a card that encodes a server-specific redirect (for example /review?s=ana) pointing to the same Google link. The site logs taps per server for coaching only.
   - Do not ask guests to mention the server. Do not set review or tap quotas. Both are explicitly banned.
   - Reward the behaviour, not the outcome: a monthly team merienda when the shift lead confirms the card was presented at every bill, and recognition for the server whose name comes up unprompted in reviews.
   - Be honest with the owners: Google does not reveal which tap became a review, so per-server attribution of actual reviews is not possible.
5. Hapag (POS) hooks, 2 to 4 days of build since Mogoba's code is reused, PHP 0 of extra software cost. Expected impact: high on speed of recovery, medium on volume.
   - (a) A receipt footer with the review QR on every receipt and bilao label.
   - (b) An optional 1-to-5 private feedback screen with "tell the manager" reasons, tied to the ticket, table and server, Toast style. It must ALWAYS show the Google link on the same screen whatever the rating. No gating.
   - (c) An owner alert by SMS or Messenger on any 1 or 2 rating, so the manager can visit the table before the guest leaves.
   - (d) After an event, reservation or bilao order is completed, one review request sent through Messenger, or by SMS at PHP 0.56 each (about PHP 35 a month for 60 messages). Only to customers who consented. No repeat.
6. Review reply routine, 15 minutes a day, PHP 0. Expected impact: medium, about 12% more reviews and +0.12 stars in the hotel study.
   - Reply to every review within 48 hours, in the language the reviewer used (Ilocano, Filipino or English).
   - Mention the dish they mentioned. Never copy-paste.
   - For negative reviews, apologise, give a name and a Messenger contact, and keep personal data out.
   - Optional: Hapag drafts a reply for the owner to approve, Popmenu style. Do not auto-post.
7. Facebook: turn on reviews and recommendations, pin a "Recommend us" post, and add the Google link to the auto-reply on Messenger. Half a day, PHP 0. Expected impact: medium for social proof in PH; Google remains primary for Maps discovery.
8. Monthly reputation snapshot in the Hapag owner dashboard, part of item 5: review count, rating, newest review age, taps per server, unanswered reviews.

What NOT to build yet:
- Paid reputation suites such as Birdeye, Podium or Yext.
- Branded stands at A$27 to A$49 each.
- Review widgets or AI auto-posting of replies.
- TripAdvisor work.
- A separate profile for Don Jose or Rosalina.
- Any discount, freebie, raffle or "show us your review" promo.
- Tablet kiosks for reviews on the premises (they risk the on-premises pressure clause).
- Review volume campaigns tied to events.

5) QUESTIONS THE OWNERS WILL LIKELY ASK
=====================================================
Q: Can we give a free cucumber lemonade for a 5-star review, or for any review?
A: No. Google bans incentives for any review, positive or not. The penalty can block new reviews, hide the existing ones or put a public warning on the profile.

Q: Can we send unhappy guests to us and happy ones to Google?
A: No, that is review gating. You can offer private feedback, but the Google link must be offered to everyone.

Q: Can we ask staff to get 10 reviews a week, or ask guests to name the server?
A: No, both are explicitly prohibited. Reward staff for asking, not for review counts.

Q: Can family and staff post reviews to get us started?
A: No. That is a conflict of interest, and it is the most common thing the filters catch.

Q: How fast will we reach 4.5?
A: About 7 months in the base case, if new reviews average 4.6 or better. Faster if the kitchen fixes recurring complaints.

Q: Why not just buy reviews?
A: Google removed 292 million reviews in 2025 and puts warning banners on offenders. One sweep can wipe out years of real reviews.

Q: Does NFC work on everyone's phone?
A: No. iPhone XS and newer work. On Android, NFC depends on the model and region and is often switched off. That is why every tag also has a QR code.

Q: What about a bad review someone made up?
A: Report it from the profile. Reply calmly and factually in public. Removal is not guaranteed and can take time.

Q: Should Don Jose Pizza and Rosalina Cafe get their own Google pages?
A: Not unless each runs as a distinct operation with its own signage and counter. Google wants one profile per business. Lucia's should get its own.

Q: Does Facebook matter more than Google here?
A: For reach and chat, yes; PH has about 95.8M social media identities. For being found on Maps along the highway, Google matters. Do both. Google comes first for reviews.

Q: What does this cost us?
A: About PHP 3,500 to 7,500 one-time for stands and cards, about PHP 35 a month for SMS, and staff habit. The POS hooks are part of Hapag.

Q: Will AI tools like ChatGPT find us?
A: Increasingly: 45% of US consumers used AI for local recommendations in 2026. Whitespark's top AI visibility factor is appearing on "best of" lists, so local food bloggers and listicles help. PH figure UNVERIFIED.

3) PITFALLS, RULES AND COMPLIANCE
==================================================
- SMS consent. The National Privacy Commission has said unsolicited SMS blasts, and collecting numbers for them, violate the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173): https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1500049/sms-pol-ads-need-users-consent (reported; direct fetch blocked). Unauthorized processing carries 1 to 3 years in prison and a ₱500,000 to ₱2,000,000 fine: https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/256486/aquino-signs-law-ensuring-confidentiality-of-personal-data. Marketing needs documented opt-in at the point of collection and a clear opt-out ("Reply STOP"), applied before the next send: https://www.telerivet.com/blog/business-sms-messaging-compliance-in-the-philippines. Never text numbers from old order books, GCash records or customers' phone contacts unless those people opted in.
- Never buy or use a "text blaster" device. They are not NTC-authorized. https://business.inquirer.net/332368/ntc-to-e-stores-stop-selling-text-blasters
- Links in SMS. Do not use shortened links, which Smart blocks silently: https://semaphore.co/advisories. Prefer no link at all ("Message us on FB: Juval's Grill"), or the full website domain sent through an A2P gateway.
- Messenger. Promotions may only go out within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Message tags cannot carry deals or discounts. Broadcasts outside the window must use paid Marketing Messages, limited to 1 per user per day. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy. "Blast all past chatters" tools that misuse tags risk Page restrictions.
- Google reviews. Do not offer a free drink, discount or raffle entry for reviews. Do not send review requests only to happy guests. Do not have staff stand over guests while they review. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114
- Facebook giveaways. Meta's Page promotion rules ban "share to enter" and "tag friends to enter." Secondary source: https://www.shortstack.com/help/facebook-promotion-guidelines/. I could not fetch Meta's own page, so this is reported, not verified.
- DTI permits. Raffles, contests and games announced through mass media need a DTI sales promotion permit: https://fairtrade.dti.gov.ph/?p=510. Filing at least 30 days ahead is reported: https://ndvlaw.com/what-rules-govern-social-media-giveaways-and-online-raffles/ (direct fetch blocked). Whether a plain fixed discount needs a permit: UNVERIFIED. Ask DTI Cagayan.
- VAT. Budget for 12% VAT on top of Meta and TikTok spend (RA 12023). https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2025/soon-taking-effect-the-12-vat-on-digital-services.html
- Small-audience fatigue. With roughly 120,000 to 175,000 reachable users nearby (UNVERIFIED estimate), a ₱30,000-a-month push shows the same people the same ad many times. Rotate creative every 2 to 3 weeks and cap the radius budget.
- "Boost post" trap. Boosting for likes buys cheap impressions, not orders. Use the Messaging objective with Messenger as the destination. https://modernchameleon.com/how-much-are-facebook-ads-in-the-philippines-the-2026-reality-check/
- Influencer disclosure. Ask paid creators to use the platform's paid-partnership label. Philippine legal requirement specifics: UNVERIFIED.
- Lucia's. Keep it as a separate Page under the same Meta Business Portfolio, with separate ad accounts or campaigns. Merging Pages destroys reviews and history. This is general Meta practice and not separately sourced.

- "Why not just boost our posts?" Boosting buys likes and views. Messenger ads buy conversations that turn into bilao orders and bookings, and we can count them.

- "Can we send promos to everyone who ever messaged our Page?" Only within 24 hours of their last message, or through Meta's paid Marketing Messages to opted-in people, once a day at most.
- "Can we give a free milk tea for a 5-star review?" No. Google can remove reviews or penalize the listing. Ask everyone the same way, with no reward.
- "Can we run a share-and-tag raffle?" No. Meta bans share-to-enter and tag-to-enter, and raffles need a DTI permit.
- "Do we really need TikTok?" Adults spend about 1.5 hours a day there, nearly the same as Facebook. Post organically. Pay only to boost a video that already works.
- "Will ads work when our area is small?" Yes, but the same people will see the ads often. That is why the budget is capped and the creative changes every 2 to 3 weeks.
- "How do we know it is working?" Through inbox labels, the "How did you hear?" question at the register, and a monthly scorecard of cost per order.
- "Is VAT added?" Yes, 12% on Meta and TikTok spend since June 2025.
- "What about Lucia's?" Use a separate Page and separate campaigns in the same Business Portfolio, and cross-promote both in the same posts and SMS when it makes sense.
- "Should we hire an agency?" Not yet. The setup is a few days of work. An agency costs more per month than the ad budget.
- "Will the new bridge bring customers?" Possibly. It carries more than 6,000 travelers a day (reported), but whether that traffic passes Juval's is UNVERIFIED. Test with a traveler ad set and the "passing by" answer at the register.

Our Mogoba flow already blocks reused reference numbers and underpayments. Keep that, and add a manager PIN above a peso threshold.
2. Personal e-wallets for business income. Collecting into an owner's personal GCash has no MDR, but the money is mixed with personal funds and BIR still expects invoices. Whether personal-account use for business sits within GCash terms is UNVERIFIED. A Maya Business or GCash for Business QR at 1.0% is cheap and clean.
3. Gateway prerequisites. PayMongo, GCash for Business and Maya Business all need DTI (or SEC) registration and BIR 2303. If the business has these, onboarding is days. If not, that is the real blocker, not the technology.
4. VAT on fees. PayMongo's pricing page says its rates exclude VAT; other sources say they include it. Budget for 12% on top until it is confirmed.
5. SC and PWD online. The site must offer a way to claim the discount, for example a checkbox plus "show ID at pickup or send in chat". Do not store ID images in the system. Verify the ID visually and record only the ID number and name required for the BIR SC/PWD sales book. This keeps sensitive data to a minimum.
6. Internet Transactions Act. Put the registered business name, physical address, phone, and a short order, cancellation and refund policy on the ordering page and footer. Prices online must match the counter, so if delivery or packaging is charged, show it before payment.
7. Messenger rules. No broadcast blasts of promos to past chatters outside the 24-hour window. The chat bubble plugin is dead, so use m.me links (the site already does).
8. Google Business Profile naming. Do not name the profile "Juval's Grill, Don Jose Pizza and Rosalina Cafe". Keep one profile named Juval's Grill & Restaurant. A separate Rosalina Cafe profile is defensible only if it operates as a distinct, publicly facing entity with its own counter and ideally its own phone. Otherwise list the brands in the menu and description.
9. Delivery promises. Own-rider delivery creates ITA delivery obligations and food-safety exposure on a highway route. Start with pickup and a fixed short-radius delivery (Camalaniugan, Aparri, Lal-lo) at a flat fee, or none.
10. Slow networks. A 535 KB hero image and 2.97 MB of photos are heavy for a provincial phone on a weak signal. Ordering pages must work after the first load even if the signal drops: cache the menu, keep the cart locally and retry on submit.
11. Vendor claims. Every Owner.com, ChowNow, Toast or Popmenu result above is the vendor's own number from US restaurants. Do not quote them to the owners as expected results.

B. BIR registration of a POS
- CRM/POS machines still need a Permit to Use (PTU). RMO 9-2021 replaced the PTU with an Acknowledgement Certificate only for CAS/CBA. It says CRM/POS registration is "different from the System's registration": https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/bir-rm-09-2021 . - RMO 24-2023 (26 Jun 2023) covers accreditation of CRMs, POS and invoicing software.
  - Applying is free.
  - The system demonstration is set within 3 working days of a complete application.

C. RR 7-2024: the invoice replaces the official receipt
- RR 7-2024 took effect 27 Apr 2024. The invoice is now the primary document for goods and services, and the OR becomes a supplementary document. On a POS, renaming "Official Receipt" to a name that includes "Invoice" is a minor change that needs no new PTU. https://batasnatin.com/laws/sales-invoice-vs-official-receipt-after-the-ease-of-paying-taxes-act-rr-7-2024 and https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2024/07/tnf-philippines-updated-invoicing-requirements.html
- RMC 77-2024 (11 Jul 2024), BIR primary source: https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2077-2024%20Digest.pdf
  - A VAT-registered seller must issue a registered VAT invoice for every sale, whatever the amount.
  - A non-VAT seller must issue one for sales of PHP 500 or more, whenever the buyer asks, and as one end-of-day invoice when the small sales add up past PHP 500.
  - A business name may be printed for branding. That matters for three brands under one registration.
- EOPT Act (RA 11976), Section 237 text: the PHP 500 threshold is adjusted every 3 years by CPI. Books and records must be kept for 5 years. https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html
- POS machines had to be reconfigured by 30 Jun 2024. Unstamped ORs issued after 31 Dec 2024 are supplementary documents only. https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/vat-invoicing-requirements-eopt-act

D. E-invoicing (EIS) timeline
- RR 11-2025 (27 Feb 2025), BIR digest: https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%2011-2025%20Digest.pdf
  - Covered now: e-commerce or internet sellers, LTS taxpayers, Large taxpayers, and CAS/CBA "and other invoicing software" users.
  - "Taxpayers using POS System" are in a LATER group. They are covered only "upon establishment by the BIR of a system", under "a separate Revenue Regulations".
  - Micro taxpayers are exempt and "may also use CAS, CRM, and POS System, in lieu of electronic invoices".
  - Setup costs get an extra income deduction of 100% for micro and small taxpayers.
- RR 26-2025 moved the first group's deadline from Mar 2026 to 31 Dec 2026: https://www.aureadalaw.com/post/bir-extends-compliance-period-for-electronic-invoicing-what-businesses-need-to-know
- Some secondary sources say POS users are already covered. The BIR digest itself puts them in the later group. Follow the digest.
- RISK FOR JUVAL'S: food delivery, and possibly orders through the website or Messenger, fall under RR 11-2025's e-commerce definition. If Juval's is Small or larger (gross sales of PHP 3M or more), its accountant must say whether online orders bring it into the 31 Dec 2026 group. UNVERIFIED.
- Micro means gross sales under PHP 3M. Small means PHP 3M to under 20M. https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/eopt-is-here-revised-rules-on-taxpayer-classification-and-reduced-penalties-for-micro-and-small-taxpayers/

- Operating a POS without a PTU: compromise penalty of PHP 25,000 (VAT) or PHP 10,000 (non-VAT), per a law-firm summary of RMO 7-2015: https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/penalty-for-lack-of-bir-permit-philippines . This is not confirmed against the RMO 7-2015 annex, so treat it as UNVERIFIED.
- Business closure for at least 5 days ("Oplan Kandado", Sec. 115) for failing to register or failing to issue invoices: https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/penalties-for-non-issuance-of-official-receipts-in-the-philippines-1
- EOPT relief for micro and small taxpayers: compromise penalties under Sec. 113, 237 and 238 are cut by at least 50%, and the civil penalty rate is 10% (RA 11976 link above).

- JMC 01-2022 extends the 20% discount and VAT exemption to online and phone or SMS orders: https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/signature-of-scs-and-pwds-on-qualified-transactions-on-online-purchases-no-longer-required/ (title says a signature is no longer required for online purchases)
- Worked example for a VAT-registered business: a PHP 500 bill becomes PHP 357.14 (divide by 1.12, then take 20% off). The discount applies to the lower of the promo price or the regular price, with no double discount. https://taxify.ph/blog/pwd-senior-citizen-discount-vat-exemption-philippines/

=== 3) PITFALLS, RULES AND COMPLIANCE ===
1. Do not let Hapag become an unregistered POS issuing receipt-like slips for taxable sales.
   - An invoice must be "duly registered". Order slips and billing statements are only supplementary documents and never replace the invoice (RMC 77-2024 link).
   - Until Hapag is accredited and each terminal has a PTU, Juval's needs another way to issue invoices: registered manual invoices printed under an ATP, or a separately registered POS.
   - Hapag prints kitchen tickets and a bill labeled as not an invoice. Ask the RDO about exact wording and whether order slips need an ATP. UNVERIFIED.
2. Sec. 264-A covers software that is "capable of" deleting or modifying sales records.
   - Production builds must have no sale delete and no editing of a closed sale.
   - Voids and refunds must be append-only entries with a reason, a PIN and a log.
   - Restoring a backup must never roll back sales history.
   - Mogoba's JSON backup and restore and its IndexedDB wipe paths must be locked down before Hapag records taxable sales.
3. One registration, three brands. The invoice carries the registered name. Brand names may appear only for branding (RMC 77-2024). Per-brand sales are an internal report, not three tax entities. If the owners registered the brands separately under DTI or BIR, each needs its own invoices. Confirm with their accountant. UNVERIFIED.
4. Lucia's.
   - If it is a branch, it needs its own branch code and COR, its own PTU for each invoicing machine and its own Z-readings (StoreHub guide link).
   - If it has a different owner TIN, it is a separate taxpayer altogether. UNVERIFIED which applies.
5. SC/PWD.
   - Never apply the discount to a whole group bill.
   - Pro-rate it, or use the MEMC base for bilao and online orders.
   - Record the name, ID number and TIN if any.
   - The VAT exemption applies only if the business is VAT-registered.
6. Service charge. If Juval's adds one (for example on events), Hapag must keep it out of revenue and produce a 100% equal-distribution report by hours worked, every 2 weeks or less.
7. EIS. Do not build BIR e-invoicing transmission now. POS users wait for a separate RR, and micro taxpayers are exempt. Check the online-orders question in section 1D first.
8. Kitchen heat. Thermal paper darkens with heat. Use an impact or well-shielded printer at the grill, and keep tablets away from the fryer.
9. Spotty internet. A purely cloud POS stops in an outage, and Hapag must never depend on the internet to send kitchen tickets.
10. The PHP 500 invoice threshold applies only to non-VAT sellers. VAT-registered sellers must invoice every sale.

=== 5) QUESTIONS THE OWNERS WILL LIKELY ASK ===
- "Is your system BIR-approved?" Not yet. Today it runs the kitchen, stock and reports. Invoices still come from your registered booklets or machine. We plan to get Hapag accredited (free under RMO 24-2023), then register each terminal.
- "Why not just use UTAK or StoreHub?" They are proven and say they are BIR-compliant, at about PHP 1,500 to 4,500 per month plus PHP 30,000 to 50,000 in hardware. Neither is built around three kitchens in one order with per-brand reports, and neither has support in Cagayan. If the BIR timing is the deciding factor, StoreHub Starter is a fair choice.
- "What happens when the internet goes down?" Orders, kitchen tickets and payments keep working on the local network. Only the owner's remote view and online orders pause.
- "What if a tablet breaks?" The data also sits on the hub and the cloud. Swap in a PHP 7,990 tablet and log in.
- "Do we give the senior discount on the whole bilao or group bill?" No. Only the senior's or PWD's own share (DOJ Opinion 45-2024; RMC 71-2022). The system computes it and logs the ID.
- "Discount on top of a promo?" Not under current law. They get the better of the two. A bill to allow both is pending.
- "Must service charge go to staff?" Only if you charge one. Then all of it goes to non-managerial staff, equally by hours, at least every 2 weeks.
- "Do we need e-invoicing by December 2026?" Probably not for a micro restaurant. POS users are scheduled later, and micro taxpayers are exempt. Ask your accountant whether online or delivery orders put you in the e-commerce group.
- "Can staff cheat it?" Voids, refunds and discounts need a manager PIN. Cash is closed blind. GCash and Maya totals are checked against the wallet app. Every change is logged, and nothing can be deleted.
- "Can one cashier sell all three brands?" Yes. One bill is issued under your registered name, and reports split sales by brand.
- "Does Lucia's need its own setup?" Its own devices, stock and Z-readings. If it is a separate branch or taxpayer, it also needs its own BIR registration.
- "Printers or screens in the kitchen?" Printers first: cheaper, heat-tolerant, no training. A screen at the café counter later, if you want timing data.

Hours, premiums and leave (Labor Code, PD 442, https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1974/pd_442_1974.html)
- Normal hours: no more than 8 a day (Art. 83). Short rest breaks during work count as hours worked (Art. 84).
- Meal period: at least 60 minutes off (Art. 85). The rule allowing a shorter paid meal break of at least 20 minutes comes from the implementing rules, which I could not open (UNVERIFIED).
- Night shift differential: at least 10% of the regular wage for each hour worked between 10 PM and 6 AM (Art. 86).
- Overtime: regular wage plus at least 25% on an ordinary day. On a holiday or rest day, at least 30% on top of that day's rate (Art. 87).
- Weekly rest: at least 24 consecutive hours after every 6 working days (Art. 91). Work on a rest day or special day earns +30%, and a special day that falls on a rest day earns +50% (Art. 93).
- Regular holiday: paid even when not worked, and double pay when worked (Art. 94). Retail and service establishments regularly employing fewer than 10 workers are exempt from holiday pay.
- Service incentive leave: 5 paid days a year after one year of service (Art. 95). Establishments with fewer than 10 employees, and employers already giving at least 5 days of vacation leave, are exempt (https://sweldoph.com/guides/workers-statutory-benefits). Unused days convert to cash (same source).
- Pay frequency: at least once every two weeks, or twice a month at intervals of no more than 16 days (Art. 103).
- Deductions: only those allowed by Art. 113 (insurance with consent, union dues, or deductions authorized by law). Art. 116 makes it unlawful to withhold wages without the worker's consent. This matters for cash shortages, breakages and "bale" (cash advances).
- Money claims prescribe after 3 years (Art. 291 in the original numbering). DOLE has visitorial power to inspect employer records at any time (Art. 128). Keep time and pay records for at least 3 years. A specific 3-year retention rule in the implementing rules (Book III Rule X) is UNVERIFIED because I could not open it.

- Ordinary day, night hour: 110%. Ordinary day overtime: 125%. Night plus overtime: 137.5%. Rest day overtime: 169%. Rest day night overtime: 185.9%. Regular holiday overtime: 260%. Regular holiday night overtime: 286%. The premiums multiply together rather than add.

  - Special day: "no work, no pay" unless company policy says otherwise. Worked: 130%. Overtime: hourly rate x 130% x 130%. On a rest day: 150%, with overtime at x 130%.
  - Regular holiday not worked: 100%, if the worker worked or was on approved leave the day before. Worked: 200%. On a rest day: 200% x 130%. Overtime on a rest day: x 130% x 130%.
- The 2027 rules are already out (Labor Advisory 17-2026, published 30 Sep 2026, https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/1004230/dole-releases-2027-holiday-pay-rules/story/). So the holiday table needs an update once a year.

Worked examples at P500 a day (P62.50 an hour), from the rules above
- 2 overtime hours on an ordinary day: 62.50 x 1.25 x 2 = P156.25.
- One hour of cleanup from 10 to 11 PM: 62.50 x 1.10 = P68.75.
- 8 hours on Dec 25: P1,000. On Dec 24 or Dec 31 (special days): P650. On the worker's rest day: P650.
- Regular holiday falling on a rest day: 500 x 2 x 1.3 = P1,300.

13th month pay
- One twelfth of the basic salary earned in the calendar year, paid no later than 24 December. Overtime and COLA are excluded. The worker must have worked at least one month in the year (PD 851, https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1975/pd_851_1975.html).
- Memorandum Order 28 (1986) extended it to all rank-and-file employees and set the 24 December deadline (https://lawphil.net/executive/mo/mo1986/mo_28_1986.html).
- The next deadline is 24 December 2026, about 11 weeks from today.

Service charge
- RA 11360 (2019) amended Art. 96. All service charges collected by restaurants go "completely and equally" to covered workers. Managerial employees are excluded. Service charges cannot be counted toward minimum wage compliance (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11360_2019.html).
- DOLE DO 242-2024, the revised IRR, says (https://www.divinalaw.com/dose-of-law/new-guidelines-for-service-charge/):
  - Covered workers include non-regular and agency workers.
  - The split is equal, based on actual hours or days worked.
  - It is paid out at least once every two weeks, or twice a month with no more than 16 days between payouts.
  - Existing benefits cannot be reduced.

Government contributions, 2025 to 2026
- SSS: 15% of the Monthly Salary Credit (MSC). Employer pays 10%, employee 5%. MSC floor is P5,000 and ceiling is P35,000. Employer also pays EC of P10 (MSC below P15,000) or P30 (P15,000 and up). This took effect January 2025 (SSS Circulars 2024-006 to 2024-010; https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/hr-payroll-alerts/sss-contribution-rates-increased-to-15). It continues unchanged in 2026, since 2025 was the final RA 11199 step (https://www.homecredit.ph/stories/sss-contribution-table-guide).
- PhilHealth: 5% of monthly basic salary, shared equally. Floor is P10,000 (P500 a month total) and ceiling is P100,000 (P5,000 total). Overtime, allowances and 13th month are excluded from the base (PhilHealth Advisory PA2025-0002, https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/advisories/2025/PA2025-0002.pdf). PIA reports "no hike in premium rates for 2026" (https://pia.gov.ph/news/no-hike-in-premium-rates-for-2026-says-philhealth/; title only, page blocked).
- Pag-IBIG: 2% from the employee and 2% from the employer, on a Maximum Fund Salary of P10,000. That is P200 plus P200 at most. Fund salary of P1,500 or less is 1% employee and 2% employer. Effective February 2024 (HDMF Circular 460, https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/hr-payroll-alerts/pag-ibig-circular-460).

Withholding tax
- Graduated table from 1 Jan 2023 (https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/philippines/individual/taxes-on-personal-income):
  - Up to P250,000: 0%.
  - P250,000 to 400,000: 15% of the excess.
  - P400,000 to 800,000: P22,500 plus 20%.
  - P800,000 to 2M: P102,500 plus 25%.
  - P2M to 8M: P402,500 plus 30%.
  - Over P8M: P2,202,500 plus 35%.
- Minimum wage earners are exempt from income tax on the statutory minimum wage, holiday pay, overtime, night differential and hazard pay (RR 8-2018, https://ebvlaw.com/?p=3350).
- 13th month and other benefits are tax-free up to P90,000. Other extra compensation paid to a minimum wage earner is taxable (https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/amendments-on-compensation-tax/).
- In practice: P500 x 313 days = P156,500 a year, well under P250,000. Most Juval's crew will have zero withholding. Supervisors earning above about P20,800 a month will need it.

What one minimum-wage worker costs per month (my arithmetic from the sources above; the SSS bracket is approximate)

- Employer adds:
  - SSS: P1,300 plus P10 EC.

Data Privacy Act (RA 10173, https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html)
- Processing must follow transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality. Data must be "adequate and not excessive" for the purpose.
- Lawful grounds include contract, legal obligation and the employer's legitimate interests. Consent is not the only basis.
- The Act's list of "sensitive personal information" covers health, IDs issued by government, and similar data. Biometrics are not named in the Act itself. How the NPC treats fingerprints and face templates is UNVERIFIED because I could not open privacy.gov.ph. The safe course is to handle them as sensitive.
- Penalties for unauthorized processing: 1 to 3 years in prison and a fine of P500,000 to P2,000,000. Improper disposal: 6 months to 2 years and P100,000 to P500,000.
- NPC registration thresholds (often quoted as 250 or more employees, or sensitive data on 1,000 or more people) are UNVERIFIED. Juval's is very likely below them, but the duty to comply applies regardless.

3) PITFALLS, RULES AND COMPLIANCE
=====================================================

1. The November 2026 wage order. A new Region II order is expected in November 2026 (https://nwpc.dole.gov.ph/region-ii/). Store the daily rate with an effective date so the old and new rates apply to the right days in a split cutoff.
2. The 10-worker line. Holiday pay and SIL exemptions apply only to establishments with fewer than 10 workers (Art. 94, Art. 95). Three kitchens, a cafe and event crew almost certainly exceed 10, so assume no exemption. How Juval's and Lucia's are counted (one employer or two, one establishment or two) is UNVERIFIED and is a question for their accountant.
3. Wage order exemptions are not assumed. Press reports mention a retail and service exemption for fewer than 10 employees, but the procedure is UNVERIFIED. Never set a rate below P500 unless the owners show a written RTWPB exemption or a BMBE certificate (RA 9178).
4. Hidden night differential. Juval's closes at 9 PM. Cleanup, closing the drawer and late events (birthdays, reunions) can run past 10 PM, and each such hour earns +10% (Art. 86). The clock must capture real clock-out times, not the scheduled close.
5. Rest days for a 7-day operation. Open daily means every worker needs 24 consecutive hours off after 6 days (Art. 91). The schedule should flag a 7th straight day.
6. Holiday dates change every year, and Islamic holidays are declared late (Eid dates came by separate proclamation in 2026). Load holidays from a yearly table the manager can edit.
7. Regular holiday pay when not worked depends on attendance the day before (Labor Advisory 13-2026). The system must look at the preceding workday, not just the holiday itself.
8. Service charge. If Juval's adds a service charge to events or catering, 100% must go to non-managerial staff, split equally by hours or days worked, at least twice a month (RA 11360, DO 242-2024). Keeping part of it, or using it to top up wages to the minimum, is not allowed.
9. Deductions for shortages, breakage and "bale". Only lawful deductions are allowed (Art. 113, Art. 116). Record cash advances as loans with a signed authorization, and never auto-deduct drawer shortages.
10. Rounding and "off the clock" prep. Capture exact minutes. Any rounding policy should be neutral or in the worker's favor. Whether PH rules specifically forbid one-sided rounding is UNVERIFIED, but it is the kind of practice that turns into money claims within the 3-year window (Art. 291).
11. Overtime approval. Have the manager approve overtime before or at the end of each shift. Whether overtime that is worked but not authorized must still be paid in the PH is UNVERIFIED in this research. Assume it must, if the employer knew and let it happen.
12. Records. Keep time logs, the payroll register, payslips and 13th month computations for at least 3 years, and keep them ready for a DOLE inspection (Art. 128, Art. 291).
13. Biometrics and photos under the Data Privacy Act:
    - Collect only what the purpose needs (the proportionality principle in RA 10173).
    - A photo snapped at the fixed counter tablet is proportionate for preventing buddy punching.
    - Continuous GPS, phone tracking, or storing face templates is not needed, because everyone clocks in on premises.
    - Give a written privacy notice.
    - Show photos only to the manager and delete them after the cutoff dispute window (suggest 60 days). That deletion period is our recommendation, not a legal number.
    - Keep everything on the device and our server, not in a third-party face recognition cloud.
14. Hardware trap. The planned Galaxy Tab A11 has no NFC (GSMArena). Either use a USB card reader on the tablet's USB-C port, or use PIN plus photo.
15. Filing is a separate legal duty. SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and BIR remittances and returns remain the owners' or their bookkeeper's job. Hapag should prepare figures, not file. A wrong automated filing is worse than none.
16. Paying wages through GCash or Maya. Whether DOLE rules allow payment to e-wallets, and under what consent conditions, is UNVERIFIED. Cash with a signed payroll sheet, or a bank deposit with consent, stays the safe default.

5) QUESTIONS THE OWNERS WILL LIKELY ASK, WITH SHORT HONEST ANSWERS
=====================================================

1. Do we really have to pay P500 a day?
Yes, for non-agriculture work in Cagayan since 5 Nov 2025 (RTWPB 2-24). The only ways below it are a written exemption from the wage board or a registered BMBE certificate. A new order is expected in November 2026, and the system will take the new rate on its effective date.

2. What does one minimum-wage worker really cost us a month?
About P16,100 for 26 days. That is the P13,000 wage plus about P3,100 for SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, 13th month and leave, before any holiday premiums.

3. Do we need a fingerprint machine?
No. A PIN plus an automatic photo on the counter tablet stops most buddy punching at no hardware cost. A card tap needs a small USB reader (under P1,000, price UNVERIFIED), because the Galaxy Tab A11 has no NFC. Fingerprint devices are possible later, but we could not confirm local prices.

4. Is taking staff photos legal?
Yes, if it is for timekeeping, staff are told in writing, only the manager sees the photos, and they are deleted after the payroll dispute window. We do not store face templates or track phones.

5. Will it compute the whole payroll and pay SSS for us?
It computes hours, overtime, night, holiday and rest day pay, the contribution lines and the 13th month, and prints payslips. You or your bookkeeper approve it and file with SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and BIR. We do not file. A filing error is your legal exposure, so a human signs off.

6. Do our crew pay income tax?
Minimum wage earners pay none on their wage, overtime, holiday and night pay. Anyone earning up to P250,000 a year owes nothing. Supervisors above that need withholding, and the register will show it.

7. Who gets the 13th month? Part-timers? New hires?
All rank-and-file staff who worked at least one month this year, at one twelfth of basic pay earned. It is due by 24 December.

8. What do we pay on Christmas and New Year's Eve?
Dec 25 and Dec 30 are regular holidays: 200% if worked, 100% if not (with the attendance rule). Dec 24 and Dec 31 are special days: 130% if worked, no pay if not, unless your policy pays.

9. Can we deduct shortages or broken plates from wages?
Only within the narrow limits of the Labor Code and with proper process. Treat cash advances as signed loans. The system will not auto-deduct shortages.

10. If we add a service charge for events, can we keep part of it?
No. All of it goes to non-managerial staff, split equally by hours worked, at least twice a month.

11. Our staff move between Juval's and Lucia's. Does that work?
Yes. Every shift records the branch, so costs land on the right business. Whether the two count as one employer for the 10-worker rules and for SSS is a question for your accountant.

12. Why not just use Sprout or PayrollHero?
You can. Sprout Payroll Starter is P15,000 a month on a one-year contract. PayrollHero is about P7,500 a month for 20 staff. Jibble is free for basic clock-in. Ours is cheaper for one restaurant, works offline, and puts labor cost next to each kitchen's sales, which the others cannot see.

13. What if the internet goes down?
Clock-ins save on the tablet and sync when the internet returns. The same applies to sales.

14. How do we know it is cutting costs?
In week one we measure today's labor percentage and how long payroll takes each cutoff. After a month we compare. We will not promise a number in advance.

3) PITFALLS, RULES AND COMPLIANCE
=====================================================
Google review policy (https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en), verbatim. Merchants may not:
- "Offer incentives - such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review".
- "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". This is review gating.
Also: "merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises", and staff review quotas are banned.
Allowed: "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives".

What this means in practice:
- A private feedback page is fine ONLY if the Google review link is shown to everyone on the same screen, whatever their score. "4 to 5 stars goes to Google, 1 to 3 goes to the manager" is gating and is banned.
- No stamp, discount or raffle entry for a review. Loyalty stamps must never depend on reviewing.
- Service recovery for an unhappy guest (an apology, a replaced dish, a voucher for the next visit) is fine. It must never be conditional on deleting or changing a review, which counts as an incentive for "revision or removal".
- Owners, staff and relatives must not review Juval's or Lucia's (conflict of interest, same policy page).

Data Privacy Act for a guest list:
- Lawful basis. Using a phone number to confirm a booking or event is "fulfillment of a contract" (Sec. 12) and needs no extra consent. Loyalty stamps, birthday greetings and promotional SMS or Messenger messages need consent (Sec. 3(b), Sec. 12). Record the consent date and source on each guest record. Use a separate unticked opt-in for marketing, not bundled with the booking.
- Privacy notice. Say what is collected, why, how long it is kept, and how to opt out, at the counter sign, on the website form and in the first message. Honor "STOP" before the next send.
- Never import numbers from old order books, GCash histories or staff phones into marketing lists. The NPC treats unsolicited SMS blasts as violations, and penalties start at PHP 500,000 (RA 10173 Sec. 25(a)).
- NPC registration. Juval's is far below 250 employees, so registration is likely NOT required on headcount. Watch the sensitive-information test: SC and PWD discount records that store government ID numbers are sensitive personal information. If Hapag stores ID numbers for 1,000 or more individuals, registration may be triggered (Circular 2022-04). Whether BIR-required SC/PWD sales-book entries count toward the 1,000 is UNVERIFIED. Ask the NPC or a lawyer. Safer design: store only what BIR requires, mask ID numbers on screen, and keep no ID photos.
- Even without registration: designate a compliance officer (Sec. 21), PIN-lock guest data, keep backups encrypted, and have a breach plan.
- Messenger: promotional follow-ups are allowed only within 24 hours of the guest's last message. Send the post-event thank-you and review request the same day or the next morning while the thread is open, or use SMS with consent.
- SMS sender: PH SMS gateways generally require a registered sender name. That requirement is UNVERIFIED in this pass; see our ads brief for Semaphore.

Loyalty pitfalls:
- SC/PWD stacking. Decide whether stamps are earned on the net amount after SC/PWD discounts. Recommended: earn on the net amount paid, and never let a loyalty reward stack on an SC/PWD-discounted item. This is our recommendation; check with their accountant.
- Liability creep. Unredeemed points pile up. Use rewards that expire (Starbucks: 90 days per voucher) and stamps that reset yearly.
- Staff abuse. Staff can stamp their own number. Require a manager PIN to add manual stamps, and audit numbers with unusual stamp rates.
- Over-messaging. With a small audience, more than 2 to 3 marketing messages a month drives opt-outs (practitioner judgment, UNVERIFIED).

Events and catering pitfalls:
- Double-booking the rattan hall when bookings live in several Messenger threads.
- No deposit, so no-shows. A 50% down payment is common practice for PH events and catering, but I could not verify a published norm (UNVERIFIED).
- No final headcount deadline, so food waste.
- Verbal menu changes not written down.
- Deposits paid to a personal GCash number with no record. Use a business QR and record each deposit as a payment against the event in Hapag (QR Ph at about 1.0% via Maya Business, https://www.maya.ph/business/pricing).
- Refund terms not stated. The Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) requires clear prices and terms for online transactions (https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/corporate-secretarial-alerts/internet-transactions-act-of-2023).

6) QUESTIONS THE OWNERS WILL LIKELY ASK (short, honest answers)
=====================================================
- "Do we need a loyalty app or cards?" No. The guest's mobile number is the card, which is what StoreHub and Loyverse do. Plastic cards and apps cost money and get lost or uninstalled.
- "Won't free items eat our margin?" At 1 stamp per PHP 300 and a PHP 400 to 450 reward after 10 stamps, the food cost is about 4.5% of what that guest spent. You only give it to people who already came back several times.
- "Can we give a discount to people who post a 5-star review?" No. Google bans incentives for reviews and asking only happy guests. We ask everyone, the same way, with no reward.
- "Can we stop bad reviews?" No, and we will not try. We give unhappy guests a fast private way to tell you, and you fix it within a day. Many will not need to post publicly, but everyone still sees the Google link.
- "Is it legal to text our customers?" Yes, for their own booking or order. For promos and birthdays, only if they said yes, with the date recorded and a way to stop. Texting numbers without consent risks fines of PHP 500,000 to 2,000,000 under the Data Privacy Act.
- "Do we need to register with the National Privacy Commission?" Probably not at your size: the thresholds are 250 employees, or sensitive data on 1,000 or more people. SC/PWD ID records could count toward the 1,000, so we will store as little as possible and you should ask your accountant or the NPC. You still need a privacy notice and a responsible person.
- "How do we stop double-booking the hall?" One events board. A date is held for 3 days, and is confirmed only when the deposit is recorded.
- "How much deposit should we ask?" We suggest 50% to confirm, with the final headcount 3 days before and the balance on the day. This is common practice, but you set the policy.
- "What will this earn us?" Honest answer: we cannot promise a number. US studies show +5 to 9% revenue per extra star for independent restaurants. As an example, a 0.3-star rise on PHP 840,000 a month would be about PHP 12,600 to 22,700 a month. One extra event or two extra bilao orders a month covers the Suki package. We will measure: returning-guest share of sales, review count and rating, and events booked.
- "What do we look at every day?" One morning message: yesterday by kitchen against last week, regulars returning, reviews waiting, events this week. Food cost and labor once a week.
- "Can Lucia's share the same list?" Yes. Stamps and records work across both, with each sale tagged by branch. Say so in the privacy notice.
- "Why not just use StoreHub or Loyverse?" You can. Loyverse loyalty is free and StoreHub starts at about PHP 2,249 a month. Hapag adds what they do not: three-kitchen routing, the events board with deposits, the Google-compliant feedback loop and offline operation built for your internet. It also reuses a system already running in Aparri.
- "Why pay you every month?" The monthly fee covers hosting and backups, review replies, posts, fixes and the monthly report. It is priced below what separate vendors charge combined. Package 1 can start without the POS.
